App Store Connect — the App Privacy questionnaire

Separate from the Privacy Policy URL, and mandatory. Apple asks, category by

category, what the app collects. The answers below are derived from the

schema, not guessed — every field the app stores is accounted for.

Where: appstoreconnect.apple.com → your app → App Privacy (left

sidebar) → Get Started.

---

The one that shapes everything

> Do you or your third-party partners use data for tracking?

> No.

Ronda has no advertising, no analytics SDK, no ad identifier, and shares

nothing with data brokers. Nothing is used to track anyone across other

companies' apps or websites. Answer No, and every "Used for Tracking" question

afterwards is also No.

---

What to tick as collected

For each, Apple asks three things: is it collected, is it **linked to the

user's identity, and what is it used for**.

Category → TypeCollectedLinkedUsed for
Contact Info → Email AddressYesYesApp Functionality
Contact Info → NameYesYesApp Functionality
Location → Coarse LocationYesYesApp Functionality
User Content → Photos or VideosYesYesApp Functionality
User Content → Other User ContentYesYesApp Functionality
Identifiers → User IDYesYesApp Functionality
Usage Data → Product InteractionYesYesAnalytics

App Functionality is the purpose for all of them except the last. Not

Product Personalisation, and not Advertising — nothing here is used to target

anything at anyone.

Notes on the awkward ones

Coarse Location. The app asks iOS for an approximate position and turns it

into a city name on the device — the coordinates never leave the phone.

But a city name *is* transmitted and stored, so it counts as collected.

Coarse, not Precise: Apple defines Precise as finer than three decimal places,

and a city centre is nowhere near that.

Name. A first name only. There is no field for a surname.

Other User Content covers messages, bios, and the private notes people

write about someone they met.

User ID. Every account has one. It is not shared with anyone.

Product Interaction, and why it is Analytics rather than App

Functionality. The app records one thing about how you use it: that you

opened an external link, with the site's domain, the screen, the city and the

time. It is not needed for anything in the app to work — it exists to answer

whether Ronda is useful to the places travellers spend money. That is

evaluating behaviour, so it is Analytics, and saying otherwise would be

choosing the flattering box.

It is Linked to identity, because the row carries the user id until the

account is deleted, at which point it is deleted too.

This is not tracking in Apple's sense: nothing is shared with a data

broker, nothing is joined to data from other apps or websites, and there is no

advertising identifier anywhere in the app. NSPrivacyTracking stays false

and App Tracking Transparency does not apply.

Browsing History was considered and left unticked. Apple defines it as

content the user viewed *outside* the app. Ronda records that a link was

tapped, inside the app; it never learns whether the page opened, what was on

it, or where the person went next.

---

What to leave unticked

Nothing in any of these is collected:

Health & Fitness · Financial Info · Contacts · Browsing History ·

Search History · Purchases · Diagnostics · Sensitive Info ·

Device ID · Advertising Data · Audio Data · Gameplay Content ·

Customer Support · Physical Address · Phone Number · Precise Location ·

Emails or Text Messages *(this means the contents of the user's own mailbox,

not messages sent inside the app)* · Other Data Types

Date of birth is worth calling out: the app asks people to confirm they

are 18, and stores only that they confirmed and when. It never asks for or

stores a birth date, so there is nothing to declare.
